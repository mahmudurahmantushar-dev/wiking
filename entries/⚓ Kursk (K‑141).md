# ⚓ Kursk (K‑141)

## 📖 Introduction
The **Kursk** was an **Oscar II‑class nuclear submarine** of the Russian Navy, commissioned in **1994**.  
It was among the largest attack submarines ever built, designed to carry long‑range cruise missiles against aircraft carriers and surface fleets.

## 🕰️ History
- **1990**: Construction begins at Sevmash shipyard in Severodvinsk.  
- **1994**: Commissioned into the Northern Fleet.  
- **12 August 2000**: Sank during a naval exercise in the Barents Sea after onboard explosions.  
- **2001**: Wreck raised from the seabed in a massive salvage operation.  

## 🔑 Characteristics
- **Class**: Oscar II nuclear‑powered cruise missile submarine.  
- **Length**: 154 m (505 ft).  
- **Beam**: 18 m (59 ft).  
- **Displacement**: ~19,400 tons submerged.  
- **Crew**: 118 sailors.  
- **Armament**: 24 P‑700 Granit cruise missiles, torpedoes.  
- **Propulsion**: Two nuclear reactors, two steam turbines.

## 🌍 Strategic Importance
- Represented Russia’s Cold War legacy of massive, heavily armed submarines.  
- Designed to counter NATO carrier strike groups.  
- Its loss highlighted the challenges facing Russia’s post‑Soviet military.

## ⚖️ The Disaster
- **Cause**: Likely torpedo explosion, followed by secondary blasts.  
- **Rescue Efforts**: Delayed response and technical failures prevented saving trapped crew.  
- **Aftermath**: Sparked criticism of Russian naval readiness and transparency.  
- **Impact**: Led to reforms in Russian submarine safety and international cooperation in rescue technology.

## 🌟 Fun Fact
The Kursk was so large that it had **two full decks** and was longer than two football fields.
