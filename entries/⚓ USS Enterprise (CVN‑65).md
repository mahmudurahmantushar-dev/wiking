# ⚓ USS Enterprise (CVN‑65)

## 📖 Introduction
The **USS Enterprise (CVN‑65)** was a landmark in naval history as the **first nuclear‑powered aircraft carrier**.  
It was the eighth U.S. ship to bear the name “Enterprise” and became one of the most iconic vessels in the U.S. Navy.

## 🕰️ History
- **1958**: Construction begins at Newport News Shipbuilding.  
- **24 September 1960**: Launched.  
- **25 November 1961**: Commissioned into the U.S. Navy.  
- **1962**: Participated in the Cuban Missile Crisis blockade.  
- **1965–1975**: Saw extensive service in the Vietnam War.  
- **1980s–1990s**: Deployed in Cold War operations and Middle East conflicts.  
- **2001**: First carrier to launch strikes in Afghanistan after 9/11.  
- **2012**: Decommissioned after 51 years of service.  
- **2022**: Officially stricken from the Naval Vessel Register.

## 🔑 Characteristics
- **Class**: Enterprise‑class nuclear aircraft carrier (only ship of its class).  
- **Length**: 1,123 ft (342 m).  
- **Beam**: 132 ft (40 m).  
- **Displacement**: ~93,000 tons full load.  
- **Propulsion**: 8 nuclear reactors, 4 shafts.  
- **Speed**: 33+ knots.  
- **Crew**: ~4,600 sailors.  
- **Air Wing**: Up to 90 aircraft.

## 🌍 Strategic Importance
- First nuclear carrier, proving the viability of nuclear propulsion for large warships.  
- Could operate for decades without refueling, giving unmatched endurance.  
- Played key roles in Cold War deterrence, Vietnam, Middle East conflicts, and post‑9/11 operations.  
- Symbolized U.S. naval power projection worldwide.

## ⚖️ Legacy
- Nicknamed the **“Big E”**, continuing a tradition from WWII’s USS Enterprise (CV‑6).  
- Served longer than any other U.S. aircraft carrier in history.  
- Paved the way for the **Nimitz‑class** and **Gerald R. Ford‑class** carriers.  
- Remembered as one of the most important warships of the 20th century.

## 🌟 Fun Fact
The Enterprise was the only carrier ever built with **eight nuclear reactors** — later designs used just two.
