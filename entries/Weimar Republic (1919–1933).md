# Weimar Republic (1919–1933)

## 📖 Introduction
The **Weimar Republic** was the German state between the end of World War I and the rise of Nazi Germany.  
It was named after the city of **Weimar**, where the constitutional assembly met in 1919.

## 🕰️ History / Timeline
- **1918**: Kaiser Wilhelm II abdicates; Germany becomes a republic.  
- **1919**: Weimar Constitution adopted; Friedrich Ebert becomes first president.  
- **1923**: Hyperinflation crisis devastates the economy.  
- **1924–1929**: Relative stability under Gustav Stresemann; cultural flourishing in Berlin.  
- **1929**: Great Depression hits; unemployment and extremism rise.  
- **1933**: Adolf Hitler appointed Chancellor; Weimar Republic ends, Nazi dictatorship begins.  

## 🔑 Key Features
- 🏛️ **Government**: Semi‑presidential republic with proportional representation.  
- 📜 **Constitution**: Guaranteed civil liberties, but Article 48 allowed presidential emergency powers.  
- 💰 **Economy**: Struggled with reparations from the Treaty of Versailles and hyperinflation.  
- 🎭 **Culture**: Golden age of German cinema, art, and science (e.g., Bauhaus, Fritz Lang’s *Metropolis*).  
- ⚖️ **Challenges**: Political violence, weak coalition governments, rise of extremist parties.  

## 🌍 Legacy / Impact
- Demonstrated both the **promise and fragility of democracy** in post‑war Europe.  
- Its collapse paved the way for **Nazi dictatorship** and World War II.  
- Still studied as a cautionary tale about economic instability and political extremism.  

## 🌟 Fun Fact
The anthem of the Weimar Republic was **“Das Lied der Deutschen”** (“The Song of the Germans”), which later became Germany’s national anthem.
