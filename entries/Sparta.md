# Sparta

## 📖 Introduction
**Sparta** (Ancient Greek: Λακεδαίμων, *Lacedaemon*) was one of the most prominent city‑states of ancient Greece.  
It was renowned for its **militaristic culture**, **dual kingship**, and strict social system.

## 🕰️ History / Origins
- **Founded**: c. 9th century BCE in Laconia, southeastern Peloponnese.  
- **Government**: Unique system with **two kings**, **Ephors** (magistrates), and the **Gerousia** (council of elders).  
- **Key Events**:  
  - **Messenian Wars (7th century BCE)** – expansion through conquest.  
  - **Battle of Thermopylae (480 BCE)** – King Leonidas and 300 Spartans against Persia.  
  - **Peloponnesian War (431–404 BCE)** – Sparta defeated Athens, becoming dominant in Greece.  
  - **Battle of Leuctra (371 BCE)** – Sparta’s power broken by Thebes.  
  - **Annexed by Achaean League (192 BCE)**.  

## 🔑 Key Features
- 🛡️ **Military Society**: Boys trained from age 7 in the *agoge* system; Spartan hoplites were elite warriors.  
- 👩 **Women’s Role**: Spartan women had more freedom and property rights compared to other Greek city‑states.  
- ⚖️ **Social Classes**:  
  - **Spartiates** – full citizens and warriors.  
  - **Perioeci** – free non‑citizens (craftsmen, traders).  
  - **Helots** – state‑owned serfs who farmed the land.  
- 🏛️ **Culture**: Valued discipline, austerity, and loyalty to the state over art and philosophy.  

## 🌍 Legacy / Impact
- Sparta became a **symbol of military excellence** and sacrifice.  
- Its society influenced later ideas of **citizenship, discipline, and martial valor**.  
- Remembered through legends like **Leonidas at Thermopylae**.  

## 🌟 Fun Fact
The phrase **“Spartan lifestyle”** comes from their reputation for simplicity and toughness.
