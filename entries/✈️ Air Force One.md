# ✈️ Air Force One

## 📖 Introduction
**Air Force One** is not a single aircraft but a **call sign** used whenever the President is aboard a U.S. Air Force plane.  
The term has become synonymous with the two highly modified **Boeing VC‑25A** aircraft (based on the 747‑200B) operated by the **89th Airlift Wing** at Joint Base Andrews.

## 🕰️ History
- **1943**: First presidential aircraft was a C‑87 Liberator Express.  
- **1945**: Franklin D. Roosevelt flew on the C‑54 *Sacred Cow*.  
- **1950s–1960s**: Dwight Eisenhower and John F. Kennedy used Lockheed Constellations and Boeing 707s.  
- **1990**: Current VC‑25A aircraft entered service under George H.W. Bush.  
- **2020s**: Replacement program underway with new Boeing 747‑8 aircraft (VC‑25B).

## 🔑 Characteristics
- **Type**: Boeing VC‑25A (modified 747‑200B).  
- **Length**: 231 ft (70 m).  
- **Wingspan**: 195 ft (59 m).  
- **Range**: Unlimited with aerial refueling.  
- **Crew**: ~26 plus presidential staff.  
- **Features**: Secure communications, medical suite, defensive countermeasures, executive offices, conference rooms, and living quarters.

## 🌍 Strategic Importance
- Serves as a **mobile White House**, allowing the President to govern from anywhere.  
- Symbol of U.S. power, prestige, and technological capability.  
- Provides unmatched security and continuity of government in crisis situations.

## ⚖️ Legacy
- Iconic in popular culture, featured in films, documentaries, and global media.  
- Represents both the practical and symbolic role of the presidency.  
- Soon to be succeeded by the **VC‑25B**, ensuring continuity into the 21st century.

## 🌟 Fun Fact
When the President is not aboard, the aircraft is referred to simply by its military designation (VC‑25A). The call sign **“Air Force One”** only applies when the President is present.
