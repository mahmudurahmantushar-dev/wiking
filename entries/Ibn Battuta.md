# Ibn Battuta

## 📖 Introduction
**Ibn Battuta** (Arabic: ابن بطوطة‎), born in **Tangier, Morocco** in 1304, was a Muslim traveler, geographer, and scholar.  
He is often called the **“Islamic Marco Polo”** for his extraordinary journeys across three continents.

## 🕰️ Life & Travels
- **1325**: Began his travels at age 21 with a pilgrimage (*Hajj*) to Mecca.  
- Over **30 years**, he visited **North Africa, the Middle East, East Africa, Central Asia, India, Southeast Asia, and China**.  
- Covered about **75,000 miles (120,000 km)**, more than any other explorer of his era.  
- Served as a **judge (qadi)** in India under the Delhi Sultanate.  
- His travels were later compiled into the book *Rihla* (“Journey”), dictated to scholar **Ibn Juzay**.  

## 🔑 Key Features
- 🌍 **Scope**: Traveled to ~40 modern countries.  
- 📖 **Work**: *A Gift to Those Who Contemplate the Wonders of Cities and the Marvels of Traveling* (commonly called *Rihla*).  
- 🕌 **Roles**: Explorer, judge, scholar, diplomat.  
- ✨ **Legacy**: Provided detailed accounts of cultures, cities, and societies in the 14th century.  

## 🌍 Legacy / Impact
- His writings are a **unique historical source** on medieval Islamic civilization.  
- Inspired later explorers and historians.  
- Demonstrated the interconnectedness of the Islamic world and beyond.  

## 🌟 Fun Fact
Ibn Battuta once served as a **judge in the Maldives**, where he criticized local customs and tried to enforce Islamic law.
