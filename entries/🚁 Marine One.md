# 🚁 Marine One

## 📖 Introduction
**Marine One** is the designation used whenever the President is aboard a U.S. Marine Corps helicopter.  
It is part of the presidential transport system, alongside **Air Force One** (fixed‑wing aircraft) and **Ground Force One** (motorcade).

## 🕰️ History
- **1957**: First use of helicopters for presidential transport under Dwight D. Eisenhower.  
- **1976**: VH‑3D Sea King introduced into service.  
- **1988**: VH‑60N White Hawk added to the fleet.  
- **2000s–present**: Modernization programs underway, including the new **VH‑92A Patriot** helicopter.

## 🔑 Characteristics
- **Operators**: Marine Helicopter Squadron One (HMX‑1).  
- **Fleet**: VH‑3D Sea King, VH‑60N White Hawk, and upcoming VH‑92A Patriot.  
- **Crew**: Marine pilots and crew, with Secret Service security.  
- **Features**: Secure communications, defensive systems, and VIP accommodations.  
- **Range**: Short‑range transport, typically within Washington, D.C. region.

## 🌍 Strategic Importance
- Provides rapid, secure transport for the President.  
- Symbol of U.S. executive mobility and military precision.  
- Often seen in iconic imagery of the President boarding or disembarking at the White House lawn.  
- Complements Air Force One for longer journeys.

## ⚖️ Legacy
- A highly visible symbol of the presidency.  
- Represents the close cooperation between the U.S. Marine Corps and the Executive Office.  
- Continues to evolve with new aircraft to meet modern security needs.

## 🌟 Fun Fact
When the President is not aboard, the helicopter uses the call sign **Marine Two** if carrying the Vice President.
