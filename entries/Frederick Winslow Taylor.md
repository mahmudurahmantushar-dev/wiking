# Frederick Winslow Taylor

## 📖 Introduction
**Frederick Winslow Taylor** was a mechanical engineer and management consultant.  
He is best remembered for developing **scientific management**, also called *Taylorism*, which revolutionized industrial practices in the early 20th century.

## 🕰️ Life & Career
- **Born**: March 20, 1856, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.  
- **Education**: Stevens Institute of Technology (BS in Mechanical Engineering).  
- **Career Start**: Began as a laborer at Midvale Steel Company, quickly rising to chief engineer.  
- **Later Work**: Consulted for Bethlehem Steel, where he introduced efficiency methods.  
- **Died**: March 21, 1915, Philadelphia.  

## 🔑 Contributions
- 📏 **Time-and-Motion Studies**: Broke tasks into small steps, measured worker output, and optimized processes.  
- ⚙️ **Scientific Management**: Advocated separating planning (management) from execution (workers).  
- 📚 **Key Work**: *The Principles of Scientific Management* (1911), voted the most influential management book by the Academy of Management in 2001.  
- 🛠️ **Standardization**: Introduced standardized tools and work methods.  
- 💡 **Piece-Rate Pay**: Encouraged incentive systems to boost productivity.  

## 🌍 Legacy / Impact
- Laid the foundation for **modern industrial engineering** and **management consulting**.  
- Influenced assembly line production and efficiency movements worldwide.  
- Criticized for treating workers as machines, but his ideas remain central to productivity studies.  

## 🌟 Fun Fact
Taylor was also a **champion tennis player**, winning the first U.S. national doubles championship in 1881.
