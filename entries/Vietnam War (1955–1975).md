# Vietnam War (1955–1975)

## 📖 Introduction
The **Vietnam War** was a Cold War conflict fought in Southeast Asia between **North Vietnam (supported by the Soviet Union, China, and other communist allies)** and **South Vietnam (supported by the United States and anti‑communist allies)**.  
It was one of the longest and most controversial wars of the 20th century, deeply shaping U.S. foreign policy and Vietnamese society.

## 🕰️ Timeline
- **1954**: Geneva Accords divide Vietnam at the **17th parallel** after French defeat at Dien Bien Phu.  
- **1955**: U.S. begins supporting South Vietnam against communist insurgency.  
- **1964**: Gulf of Tonkin incident leads to large‑scale U.S. military involvement.  
- **1968**: Tet Offensive shocks U.S. public opinion, showing the war’s intensity.  
- **1973**: Paris Peace Accords signed; U.S. withdraws combat troops.  
- **1975**: Fall of Saigon; North Vietnam achieves victory, reunifying the country under communist rule.

## 🔑 Causes
- Cold War rivalry between communism and capitalism.  
- North Vietnam’s goal of reunification under communist leadership.  
- U.S. policy of **containment**, seeking to stop the spread of communism in Asia.

## 🌍 Consequences
- **Casualties**: Estimated 2–3 million Vietnamese and over 58,000 U.S. soldiers killed.  
- **Vietnam**: Reunified under communist rule; long period of economic hardship followed.  
- **United States**: Deep political divisions, anti‑war protests, and a shift in foreign policy toward caution in military interventions (“Vietnam Syndrome”).  
- **Global Impact**: Strengthened Cold War tensions, influenced later conflicts and diplomacy.

## ⚖️ Deterrence vs. Compellence
- The war showed limits of **deterrence**: U.S. nuclear superiority did not prevent a prolonged guerrilla conflict.  
- It was more about **compellence**: the U.S. tried to force North Vietnam to stop supporting insurgency in the South, but failed.  
- Ultimately, deterrence worked against nuclear escalation, but not against unconventional warfare.

## 🌟 Fun Fact
The Vietnam War was the first conflict to be **televised extensively**, bringing graphic images of combat into living rooms and fueling public opposition.
