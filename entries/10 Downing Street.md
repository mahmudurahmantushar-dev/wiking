# 10 Downing Street

## 📖 Introduction
**10 Downing Street** (often called *Number 10*) is one of the most famous political addresses in the world.  
It serves as both the **home** and **executive office** of the UK Prime Minister.

## 🕰️ History / Origins
- **Built**: 1682–1684 by Sir George Downing.  
- **Official Residence Since**: 1735, when King George II gave it to Sir Robert Walpole, Britain’s first Prime Minister.  
- **Architectural Style**: Georgian.  
- **Location**: City of Westminster, London.  

## 🔑 Key Features
- 🚪 **Iconic Black Door**: Symbol of British politics, guarded and photographed during major announcements.  
- 🏠 **Residence & Office**: Houses the Prime Minister and family, plus offices for staff.  
- 🏛️ **Cabinet Room**: Where historic decisions like WWII strategy and Brexit negotiations were made.  
- 🐈 **Larry the Cat**: The official “Chief Mouser to the Cabinet Office.”  

## 🌍 Legacy / Impact
- Directed **World War I & II strategies**.  
- Hosted leaders from Churchill to Thatcher, Blair, and beyond.  
- Modern decisions on the **economy, welfare, and foreign policy** continue to be made here.  
- Vies with the **White House** as one of the most important political buildings globally.  

## 🌟 Fun Fact
The black door at Number 10 is actually **reinforced steel**, though it looks wooden. The original wooden door was replaced for security in the 1990s.
