# Athens

## 📖 Introduction
**Athens** is one of the most famous city‑states of ancient Greece, celebrated as the **birthplace of democracy** and a center of **philosophy, art, and science**.  
It rivaled Sparta in power but was known more for its cultural achievements than military dominance.

## 🕰️ History / Origins
- **Founded**: c. 3000 BCE, continuously inhabited since the Neolithic age.  
- **Government**: Transitioned from monarchy → aristocracy → democracy (5th century BCE).  
- **Golden Age**: Under **Pericles (5th century BCE)**, Athens flourished in politics, art, and architecture.  
- **Key Events**:  
  - **Persian Wars (490–479 BCE)** – Athens played a decisive role at Marathon and Salamis.  
  - **Peloponnesian War (431–404 BCE)** – Athens was defeated by Sparta.  
  - Later absorbed into the Macedonian and Roman empires.  

## 🔑 Key Features
- 🏛️ **Democracy**: Citizens voted directly on laws in the Assembly.  
- 🎨 **Culture**: Home to philosophers (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle), playwrights (Sophocles, Euripides), and historians (Herodotus, Thucydides).  
- 🏗️ **Architecture**: Built the **Parthenon** and other monuments on the Acropolis.  
- ⚖️ **Society**:  
  - **Citizens** – free men with political rights.  
  - **Metics** – resident foreigners with limited rights.  
  - **Slaves** – no rights, formed a large part of the population.  
- 📚 **Education**: Emphasized philosophy, rhetoric, and arts, unlike Sparta’s military focus.  

## 🌍 Legacy / Impact
- Athens’ democratic ideals influenced modern political systems.  
- Its philosophers shaped Western thought for centuries.  
- Its art and architecture remain iconic symbols of classical civilization.  

## 🌟 Fun Fact
The word **“school”** comes from the Greek *scholē*, meaning “leisure” — reflecting Athens’ belief that education was a noble pursuit.
