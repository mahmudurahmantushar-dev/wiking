# ⚔️ Korean War (1950–1953)

## 📖 Introduction
The **Korean War** was the first major armed conflict of the **Cold War**, fought on the Korean Peninsula between **North Korea (DPRK)** and **South Korea (ROK)**, with heavy involvement from global powers.  
It remains technically unresolved, as no peace treaty was signed — only an armistice.

## 🕰️ Timeline
- **25 June 1950**: North Korean forces crossed the **38th parallel**, invading South Korea.  
- **July 1950**: U.S. and UN forces intervened to support South Korea.  
- **October 1950**: UN forces advanced toward the Yalu River; China entered the war to aid North Korea.  
- **1951–1953**: Stalemate around the 38th parallel, heavy fighting but little territorial change.  
- **27 July 1953**: Armistice signed at Panmunjom, establishing the **Demilitarized Zone (DMZ)**.

## 🔑 Causes
- Division of Korea after World War II: Soviet Union occupied the north, U.S. occupied the south.  
- Ideological conflict: Communism vs. Western democracy.  
- Cold War tensions between the U.S. and USSR.

## 🌍 Consequences
- **Casualties**: Estimated 2.5 million military and civilian deaths.  
- **Division**: Korea remains split into North and South, with the DMZ as a buffer.  
- **Cold War Impact**: Cemented U.S. military presence in Asia; deepened East–West rivalry.  
- **Legacy**: Often called the “Forgotten War” in the U.S., but central to Korean identity and geopolitics.

## ⚖️ Deterrence Connection
The Korean War highlighted the limits of **deterrence** during the Cold War.  
Despite nuclear threats, conventional war broke out, showing that deterrence worked better against nuclear escalation than against regional conflicts.

## 🌟 Fun Fact
The Korean War was the first conflict in which **jet aircraft** were used extensively in combat.
