# ⚓ Typhoon‑class Submarine (Project 941 Akula)

## 📖 Introduction
The **Typhoon‑class** (Russian: Проект 941 "Акула") was a series of nuclear‑powered ballistic missile submarines built by the Soviet Union in the 1980s.  
They remain the **largest submarines in history**, designed to ensure second‑strike nuclear capability against the United States.

## 🕰️ History
- **1976**: Construction begins at Sevmash shipyard in Severodvinsk.  
- **1981**: First Typhoon commissioned into the Soviet Navy.  
- **Cold War Role**: Served as a strategic deterrent, patrolling Arctic waters.  
- **1990s**: Post‑Soviet Russia decommissions most Typhoons due to cost and arms reduction treaties.  
- **2021**: Last active Typhoon (*Dmitry Donskoy*) retired, ending the class’s service.

## 🔑 Characteristics
- **Length**: 175 m (574 ft).  
- **Beam**: 23 m (75 ft).  
- **Displacement**: ~48,000 tons submerged.  
- **Crew**: ~160 sailors.  
- **Propulsion**: Two nuclear reactors, two steam turbines.  
- **Armament**: 20 R‑39 Rif intercontinental ballistic missiles (ICBMs), plus torpedoes.  
- **Design**: Double‑hull structure with multiple pressure hulls, enhancing survivability.

## 🌍 Strategic Importance
- Provided the USSR with a **credible nuclear deterrent** during the Cold War.  
- Could remain submerged for months, hiding under Arctic ice.  
- Symbolized Soviet engineering ambition and military might.  
- Their sheer size allowed for crew comfort, including amenities like a swimming pool and sauna.

## ⚖️ Legacy
- Largest submarines ever constructed.  
- Represented the peak of Cold War submarine design.  
- Though retired, they remain iconic in naval history and popular culture (e.g., *The Hunt for Red October*).

## 🌟 Fun Fact
The Typhoon‑class was so massive that each submarine contained **two parallel pressure hulls**, making them wider than most aircraft carriers.
